7.2. Geodesics#

A worldline is the a one-dimensional curve describing the trajectory of a point in the four-dimensional spacetime. Events in the four-dimensional spacetime can be parametrized with a set of coordinates \(\{ q^\mu \}\) as \(X( q^{\mu} )\).1 A worldine \(\ell\) can be represented with the 1-parameter equation \(X_{\ell}(\lambda) = X\left( q^{\mu}_{\ell}(\lambda) \right)\).

The line element in space time can be defined as \(d \mathbf{X} = d \lambda \, \partial_\lambda q^{\mu} \, \partial_{\mu} X\) \(= d \lambda \, \partial_\lambda q^{\mu} \, \mathbf{b}_{\mu}\), with \(\mathbf{b}_{\mu} = \partial_{\mu} X\) the vectors of the natural basis induced by the parametrization of the spacetime. The magnitude of the line element reads

\[ds^2 = d \mathbf{X} \cdot d \mathbf{X} = d\lambda^2 \, g_{\mu \nu}\left(q^\varphi(\lambda)\right) \, \partial_\lambda q^{\mu}(\lambda) \, \partial_\lambda q^{\nu}(\lambda) \ ,\]

with \(g_{\mu \nu} := \mathbf{b}_{\mu} \cdot \mathbf{b}_{\nu}\) the components of the metric tensor in the natural basis induced by the parametrization of space.

7.2.1. Geodesics equation#

A geodesics between two events \(P_0\), \(P_1\) in spacetime is the worldline connecting the events for which the \(\Delta s = \int_{P_0}^{P_1} ds\) is stationary. Using a parameters \(\lambda\) that’s affine with the arc-length, the equation of the geodesics reads

\[\ddot{q}^{\mu} + \Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu \sigma} \dot{q}^\nu \dot{q}^\sigma = 0 \ .\]

Defining the 4-velocity of a point \(P\) in spacetime as w.r.t. the affine parameter \(\lambda\) - remark: an affine parameter just differs from the arc-length by an offset and a scale parameter, like a different choice of unit of measurements,

\[\mathbf{U} = \partial_\lambda X\left( q^\mu(\lambda) \right) = \partial_\lambda q^{\mu} \, \partial_{q^{\mu}} X = \dot{q}^{\mu} \mathbf{b}_{\mu} = U^{\mu} \mathbf{b}_{\mu} \ ,\]

it readily follows that a geodesics is a worldine s.t. the acceleration on that orbit is zero, \(\partial_\lambda \mathbf{U} = \mathbf{0}\), as the expression of the acceleration induced by the choice of the coordinates \(\{ q^\mu \}\) is

\[\mathbf{A} := \partial_\lambda \mathbf{U} = \partial_\lambda \left( \dot{q}^\mu \mathbf{b}_{\mu} \right) = \left\{ \ddot{q}^{\mu} + \Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu\sigma} \dot{q}^\nu \dot{q}^\sigma \right\} \mathbf{b}_{\mu} \ .\]
Geodesics from variational principles

Or move this to Math: Calculus of variations: examples

\[\begin{aligned} 0 & = \delta \int_{\lambda_0}^{\lambda_1} ds = \delta \int_{\lambda_0}^{\lambda_1} \sqrt{ g_{\mu \nu}(q^\varphi(\lambda)) \, \dot{q}^{\mu}(\lambda) \dot{q}^{\nu}(\lambda) } \, d \lambda = \delta \int_{\lambda_0}^{\lambda_1} \dot{s}(\lambda) \, d \lambda \end{aligned}\]

Lagrange equation reads

\[\frac{d}{d \lambda} \dfrac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{q}^\xi} - \dfrac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q^\xi} = 0 \ .\]

The evaluation of the derivatives gives

\[\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q^\xi} = \frac{1}{2} \left( g_{\mu \nu} \dot{q}^{\mu} \dot{q}^{\nu} \right)^{-\frac{1}{2}} \partial_{\xi} g_{\mu \nu} \dot{q}^{\mu} \dot{q}^{\nu} = \frac{1}{2} \dot{s}^{-1} \partial_{\xi} g_{\mu \nu} \dot{q}^{\mu} \dot{q}^{\nu}\]
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{d \lambda} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{q}^\xi} & = \dfrac{d}{d\lambda} \left[ \frac{1}{2} \left( g_{\sigma \eta} \dot{q}^{\sigma} \dot{q}^{\eta} \right)^{-\frac{1}{2}} \left( g_{\xi \nu} \dot{q}^{\nu} + g_{\mu \xi} \dot{q}^{\mu} \right) \right] = \\ & = \dfrac{d}{d\lambda} \left[ \dot{s}^{-1} \, g_{\xi \nu} \, \dot{q}^{\nu} \right] = \\ & = \left( \dot{q}^\zeta \partial_{q^\zeta} \dot{s}^{-1} + \ddot{q}^\zeta \partial_{\dot{q}^\zeta} \dot{s}^{-1} \right) g_{\xi \nu} \dot{q}^{\nu} + \dot{s}^{-1} \left( \dot{q}^{\zeta} \partial_{\zeta} g_{\xi \nu} \dot{q}^{\nu} + g_{\xi \nu} \ddot{q}^{\nu} \right) = \\ & = - \dot{s}^{-2} \ddot{s} \, g_{\xi \nu} \dot{q}^{\nu} + \dot{s}^{-1} \left( \partial_{\zeta} g_{\xi \nu} \dot{q}^{\nu} \dot{q}^{\zeta} + g_{\xi \nu} \ddot{q}^{\nu} \right) \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

Putting everything together,

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} 0 & = \dfrac{d}{d \lambda} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{q}^\xi} - \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q^\xi} = \\ & = - \dot{s}^{-2} \ddot{s} \, g_{\xi \mu} \dot{q}^{\mu} + \dot{s}^{-1} \left( \partial_{\mu} g_{\xi \nu} \dot{q}^{\mu} \dot{q}^{\nu} + g_{\xi \mu} \ddot{q}^{\mu} \right) - \frac{1}{2} \dot{s}^{-1} \partial_{\xi} g_{\mu \nu} \dot{q}^{\mu} \dot{q}^{\nu} = \\ & = - \dot{s}^{-2} \ddot{s} \, g_{\xi \mu} \dot{q}^{\mu} + \dot{s}^{-1} \left[ g_{\xi \mu} \ddot{q}^{\mu} + \left( \partial_{\mu} g_{\xi \nu} - \frac{1}{2} \partial_{\xi} g_{\mu \nu} \right) \dot{q}^{\mu} \dot{q}^{\nu} \right] = \\ & = - \dot{s}^{-2} \ddot{s} \, g_{\xi \mu} \dot{q}^{\mu} + \dot{s}^{-1} \left\{ \, g_{\xi \mu} \ddot{q}^{\mu} + \left( \Gamma^{\sigma}_{\mu \xi} g_{\sigma \nu} + \Gamma^{\sigma}_{\mu \nu} g_{\xi \sigma} - \frac{1}{2} \Gamma_{\xi \mu}^{\sigma} g_{\sigma \nu} - \frac{1}{2} \Gamma_{\xi \nu}^{\sigma} g_{\sigma \mu} \right) \dot{q}^{\mu} \dot{q}^{\nu} \right\} = \\ & = - \dot{s}^{-2} \ddot{s} \, g_{\xi \mu} \dot{q}^{\mu} + \dot{s}^{-1} \left\{ \, g_{\xi \mu} \ddot{q}^{\mu} + \Gamma^{\sigma}_{\mu \nu} g_{\xi \sigma} \dot{q}^{\mu} \dot{q}^{\nu} \right\} \ , \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

or rearranging the indices

\[\ddot{q}^{\mu} + \Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu \sigma} \dot{q}^\nu \dot{q}^\sigma = \dfrac{\ddot{s}}{\dot{s}^3} \dot{q}^{\mu} \ .\]

If \(\lambda\) is an affine parameter, so that its relation with the arc-length is \(\lambda = a s + b\), with \(a\), \(b\) constant, then \(\ddot{s} = 0\), and the \(\mu^{\rm th}\) components of the geodesics equation reads

\[\ddot{q}^{\mu} + \Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu \sigma} \dot{q}^{\nu} \dot{q}^{\sigma} = 0 \ .\]
Useful results for derivatives and manipulations
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \partial \dot{s}^2 & = 2 \dot{s} \partial \dot{s} \\ \partial \dot{s}^n & = n \dot{s}^{n-1} \partial \dot{s} = \frac{n}{2} \dot{s}^{n-2} \partial \dot{s}^2 \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \partial_{q^{\xi}} \dot{s}^2 & = \partial_{q^\xi} \left( g_{\mu \nu} \dot{q}^{\mu} \dot{q}^{\nu} \right) = \partial_{\xi} g_{\mu \nu} \dot{q}^\mu \dot{q}^\nu \\ \partial_{\dot{q}^{\xi}} \dot{s}^2 & = \partial_{\dot{q}^\xi} \left( g_{\mu \nu} \dot{q}^{\mu} \dot{q}^{\nu} \right) = g_{\mu \xi} \dot{q}^\mu + g_{\xi \nu} \dot{q}^\nu = 2 g_{\xi \mu} q^{\mu} \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
\[\partial_{\xi} g_{\mu \nu} = \Gamma_{\xi \mu}^{\sigma} g_{\sigma \nu} + \Gamma_{\xi \nu}^{\sigma} g_{\sigma \mu}\]

7.2.2. Geodesics deviation equation#

Let a region of spacetime be parametrized by two parameters, \(\mathbf{X}\left( q^{\mu}(\lambda, s) \right)\), so that

  • geodesics are constant-\(s\) lines parametrized by \(\lambda\), \(\mathbf{X}(\lambda, \bar{s})\). The velocity vector is

    \[\mathbf{U} = \partial_{\lambda} \mathbf{X} = \partial_{\lambda} q^{\mu} \mathbf{b}_{\mu} = U^{\mu} \mathbf{b}_{\mu}\]

  • two neighboring geodesics are separated by

    \[\Delta \mathbf{X} = \mathbf{X}(\lambda, s+ds) - \mathbf{X}(\lambda, s) \sim ds \, \partial_s \mathbf{X} = ds \, \partial_s q^{\mu} \, \partial_{\mu} \mathbf{X} = ds \, \xi^{\mu} \mathbf{b}_{\mu} \ .\]

Since mixed partial derivatives of a multi-variable function are equal, and \(U^{\mu} = \partial_{\lambda} q^{\mu}\), \(\xi^{\mu} = \partial_s q^{\mu}\), then

\[\partial_s U^{\mu} = \partial_\lambda \xi^{\mu} \ .\]

7.2.2.1. Velocity of the geodesics deviation#

The evolution of the deviation elementary “segment” \(\Delta \mathbf{X} = \Delta s \, \partial_s q^{\mu} \mathbf{b}_{\mu}\) follows from the comparison of two neighboring geodesics. The velocity of the deviation is

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \Delta \mathbf{U} & := \partial_\lambda \Delta \mathbf{X} = \\ & = \partial_\lambda q^{\mu}(\lambda,s+ds) \mathbf{b}_{\mu}(\lambda,s+ds) - \partial_\lambda q^{\mu}(\lambda,s) \mathbf{b}_{\mu}(\lambda,s) = \\ & = \Delta s \left\{ \partial_{\lambda s} q^{\mu} + \Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu \sigma} \dfrac{\partial q^{\nu}}{\partial s} \dfrac{\partial q^{\sigma}}{\partial \lambda} \right\} \mathbf{b}_{\mu} + o\left(\Delta s\right) \ , \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

with \(\partial_s \mathbf{b}_{\mu} = \partial_s q^{\nu} \partial_{\nu} \mathbf{b}_{\mu} = \partial_s q^{\nu} \Gamma^{\sigma}_{\nu \mu} \mathbf{b}_{\sigma}\).

7.2.2.2. Acceleration of the geodesics deviation#

The acceleration of the deviation is

\[\Delta \mathbf{A} = \Delta s \, R^{\rho}_{\ \ \sigma \mu \nu} U^{\sigma} U^{\mu} \xi^{\nu} \, \mathbf{b}_{\rho} + o(\Delta s) \ ,\]

with \(R^{\rho}_{\ \ \sigma \mu \nu}\) the components of the Riemann tensor in the natural basis induced by the coordinates \(q^{\mu}\).

Details

The acceleration of the deviation

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \Delta \mathbf{A} & := \partial_{\lambda} \Delta \mathbf{U} = \\ & = \Delta s \Big\{ \underbrace{{\color{red}\partial_{\lambda \lambda s} q^{\mu}}}_{\partial_s \partial_{\lambda \lambda} q^{\mu}} + \partial_{\lambda} \Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu \sigma} \partial_s q^{\nu} \partial_\lambda q^{\sigma} + \Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu \sigma} \partial_{\lambda s} q^{\nu} \partial_\lambda q^{\sigma} + \Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu \sigma} \partial_s q^{\nu} \underbrace{ {\color{blue} \partial_{\lambda \lambda} q^{\sigma} } }_{-\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\xi \chi} \partial_\lambda q^{\xi} \partial_\lambda q^{\chi}, \ \text{geodesics}} + \\ & \qquad \ \ + \left. \partial_{\lambda s} q^{\chi} \partial_\lambda q^{\nu} \Gamma^{\mu}_{\chi \nu} + \partial_s q^{\nu} \partial_\lambda q^{\sigma} \partial_{\lambda} q^{\xi} \Gamma^{\chi}_{\nu \sigma} \Gamma^{\mu}_{\xi \chi} \right\} \mathbf{b}_{\mu} + o(\Delta s) = && (1) \\ & = \Delta s \Big\{ {\color{red} - \partial_{s} \Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu \sigma} \partial_\lambda q^{\nu} \partial_\lambda q^{\sigma} } {\color{red} - } \underbrace{{\color{red} \Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu \sigma} \partial_{\lambda s} q^{\nu} \partial_\lambda q^{\sigma}}}_{1} {\color{red} - } \underbrace{{\color{red} \Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu \sigma} \partial_\lambda q^{\nu} \partial_{\lambda s} q^{\sigma}}}_{2} + \\ & \qquad \quad + \left. \partial_{\lambda} \Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu \sigma} \partial_s q^{\nu} \partial_\lambda q^{\sigma} + \underbrace{ \Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu \sigma} \partial_{\lambda s} q^{\nu} \partial_\lambda q^{\sigma} }_{1} {\color{blue} - } \Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu \sigma} {\color{blue} \Gamma^{\sigma}_{\xi \chi} \partial_\lambda q^{\xi} \partial_\lambda q^{\chi} } \partial_s q^{\nu} \right. + \\ & \qquad \quad + \left. \underbrace{ \partial_{\lambda s} q^{\sigma} \partial_\lambda q^{\nu} \Gamma^{\mu}_{\sigma \nu} }_{2} + \partial_s q^{\nu} \partial_\lambda q^{\sigma} \partial_{\lambda} q^{\xi} \Gamma^{\chi}_{\nu \sigma} \Gamma^{\mu}_{\xi \chi} \right\} \mathbf{b}_{\mu} + o(\Delta s) = && (2) \\ & = \Delta s \left\{ - \partial_{\sigma} \Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu \nu} + \partial_{\mu} \Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu \sigma} - \Gamma^{\rho}_{\sigma \lambda} \Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu \nu} + \Gamma^{\lambda}_{\sigma \mu} \Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu \lambda} \right\} \, \partial_s q^{\sigma} \partial_{\lambda} q^{\mu} \partial_{\lambda} q^{\nu} \, \mathbf{b}_{\rho} + o(\Delta s) = && (3) \\ & = \Delta s \left\{ \partial_{\mu} \Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu \sigma} - \partial_{\nu} \Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu \sigma} + \Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu \lambda} \Gamma^{\lambda}_{\nu \sigma} - \Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu \lambda} \Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu \sigma} \right\} \, \partial_s q^{\nu} \partial_{\lambda} q^{\mu} \partial_{\lambda} q^{\sigma} \, \mathbf{b}_{\rho} + o(\Delta s) \ , \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

since \((1)\) the geodesics equation reads \(\partial_{\lambda \lambda} q^{\mu} = - \Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu \sigma} \partial_\lambda q^{\nu} \partial_\lambda q^{\sigma}\); \((2)\) \(\partial_s \Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu \sigma} = \partial_s q^{\chi} \partial_{\chi} \Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu \sigma}\); \((3)\) symmetry allows swapping some indices to make the components of the Riemann tensor in the natural basis appear

\[R^{\rho}_{\ \ \sigma \mu \nu} = \partial_{\mu} \Gamma^{\rho}_{\ \ \nu \sigma} - \partial_{\nu} \Gamma^{\rho}_{\ \ \mu \sigma} + \Gamma^{\rho}_{\ \ \mu \lambda} \Gamma^{\lambda}_{\ \ \nu \sigma} - \Gamma^{\rho}_{\ \ \nu \lambda} \Gamma^{\lambda}_{\ \ \mu \sigma} \ .\]
1

The function \(X( q^{\mu} )\) represents the events in the spacetime as points on a manifold. In general events can’t be represented as a (position) vector if the space is not flat, or if the \(n\)-dimensional manifold is not embedded in a \(n+1\)-dimensional space.
