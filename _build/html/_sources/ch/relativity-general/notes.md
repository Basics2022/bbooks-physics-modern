# General Relativity - Notes

As GR is a geometrical theory of gravitation, it's better to recall some [differential geometry](relativity-general:notes:differential-geometry) first. Then Einstein equivalence principle is translated in the natural motion of entities (both massive and massless, see the examples on [planetary orbits]() and [light bending]() in Schwarzschild metrics) along [geodesics](relativity-general:notes:geodesics). Geodesic deviation provides the first touch of the global effects of gravitation. The physical intuition and derivation of [Einstein field equation](relativity-general:notes:einstein-equation) follows, along with the [classical limit](relativity-general:notes:einstein-equation:classical-limit) for weak gravitation and $v/c \ll 1$. Properties of EFE are discussed **todo**. Linearization follows. Linearization of EFE gives a [wave equation](relativity-general:notes:einstein-equation:linearization) for the metric perturbations with $c$ as the characteristic speed. Linearization of the metrics and geodesics about an event in spacetime allows to describe local behavior, and recover Einstein equivalence principle with some additional details. The last section deals with [Schwarzschild metric](relativity-general:notes:examples:schwarzschild) as an analytical solution of the EFE, describing the spacetime around a massive object: this section is a first worked example of the theory developed before, allowing us to discuss some of the main effects accurately described by GR used as early experimental validations of GR theory ([light bending](relativity-general:notes:examples:deflection-of-ray-of-light) and [precession of perihelion of Mercury](relativity-general:notes:examples:schwarzschild-orbits)), and predict the existence of some unexpected entities, like black holes.

* **Equivalence principle** and free fall as the natural state of motion: the physics in a freely falling laboratory is locally undistinguishable from physics in a gravity-free inertial frame in special relativity.

* [**Einstein field equation**](relativity-general:notes:einstein-equation). EFE is derived as the simplest linear relation between Ricci tensor - a second-order curvature tensor - and the energy momentum tensor, satisfying the energy-momentum conservation relation. [Linearization of Einstein equation](relativity-general:notes:einstein-equation:linearization) is performed to show the finite velocity of propagation: in the limit of weak gravitation, EFE can be recast as a linear wave equation with characteristic speed $c$.

* Examples:
  * Schwarzschild metrics
    * orbit of a planet, and precession of perihelion
    * trajectory of a massless particle (zero-rest-mass), and bending of rays of light
