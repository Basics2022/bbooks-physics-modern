1. **Fock space** is built from commutators (bosons) or anticommutators (fermions) of creation/annihilation operators; Pauli exclusion is the algebraic fact $(c^\dagger)^2=0$, and one-body operators are $\sum t_{ij}a_i^\dagger a_j$ for both statistics.
2. **Symmetries** act by unitary operators $e^{-i\theta T}$ generated by one-body operators; their eigenvalues are additive over occupied modes. Number conservation of electrons (global $U(1)$) versus the absence of such a charge for the photon explains why photons are created singly.
3. **Local $U(1)$ gauge invariance** fixes the electron–photon coupling $\mathbf p\to\mathbf p+e\mathbf A$, producing both $H_1\propto\mathbf A\!\cdot\!\mathbf p$ and the seagull $H_2\propto\mathbf A^2$ with locked coefficients.
4. **The photon field** is a set of oscillators with $[a,a^\dagger]=1$; the classical limit is the coherent state with $\Delta E/E=1/(2\sqrt{\bar n})$, and radiation by a classical current produces exactly that state.
5. **The electron field** obeys the Schrödinger equation as an operator equation. Free and bound electrons are two different bases of one and the same field, related by a unitary transformation.
6. **Emission and absorption**: matrix elements $\propto\sqrt{n}$ and $\sqrt{n+1}$ give stimulated and spontaneous emission; in the dipole approximation $\Gamma=\omega^3|\mathbf d|^2/(3\pi\varepsilon_0\hbar c^3)$, which reproduces the measured hydrogen $2p$ lifetime of 1.6 ns, equals (oscillator strength) × (classical radiation-damping rate), and enforces Einstein's thermal balance.
7. **Free electrons cannot absorb a single photon**; the lowest-order free-electron process is Compton scattering. In the non-relativistic theory only the seagull contributes for an electron at rest, giving the Thomson cross-section $\sigma_{\rm T}=\tfrac{8\pi}{3}r_e^2$ and the Compton shift to leading order in $\hbar\omega/mc^2$; the relativistic Klein–Nishina result requires the Dirac field.
