24.1. Harmonic Oscillator#

Harmonic oscillator is used as the fundamental element in field quantization.

24.1.1. Classical model#

The Hamiltonian function of a harmonic oscillator is

\[ H=\frac{p^2}{2m}+\frac{m\omega^2x^2}{2} \ . \]

24.1.2. From classical to quantum#

Promoting generalized coordinate and momentum to operators, the Hamiltonian operator of the quantum harmonic oscillator reads

\[ \hat{H} = \frac{\hat p^2}{2m}+\frac{m\omega^2\hat x^2}{2} \ . \]

24.1.2.1. Stationary states#

The stationary states are defined as the eigenstates of the Hamiltonian operators, whose energy level is the corresponding eigenvalue

\[E_n|\psi_n\rangle=\left(\frac{\hat p^2}{2m}+\frac{m\omega^2\hat x^2}{2}\right)|\psi_n\rangle \ ,\]

or in position representation, with decaying wave function at infinity,

\[\begin{split}\left\{\begin{aligned} & E_n\psi_n=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi_n''+\frac{m\omega^2x^2}{2}\psi_n \\ & \psi\big|_{\pm\infty} \to 0 \ . \end{aligned}\right.\end{split}\]

A solution exists only for a set of discrete values of \(E_n\), while for all the other values the b.c. are not satisfied. The feasible solutions of the eigenproblem are

\[ \psi_n(x)=c_nH_n(x)e^{-\frac12x^2},\qquad E_n=\left(\tfrac12+n\right)\hbar\omega \]

\(n\)

\(\psi_n(x)\)

\(E_n\)

0

\(\psi_0=c_0e^{-x^2/2}\)

\(\tfrac12\hbar\omega\)

1

\(\psi_1=c_1x\,e^{-x^2/2}\)

\(\tfrac32\hbar\omega\)

2

\(\psi_2=c_2(2x^2-1)e^{-x^2/2}\)

\(\tfrac52\hbar\omega\)

…

\(\dots\)

\(\dots\)

Important

The lowest energy is \(\neq0\) because of Heisenberg uncertainty, i.e. a particle can’t be exactly in \(x=0\) with \(p=0\)!

Details of the spectral decomposition of the Hamiltonian operator

Let \(\xi = a x\). Then, as \(\frac{d}{dx} = \frac{d \xi}{d x} \frac{d}{d \xi} = a \frac{d}{d \xi}\) the equation can be recast as

\[E_n \psi_n(\xi) = - a^2 \frac{\hbar^2}{2 m} \psi''_n(\xi) + \frac{m \omega^2 \xi^2}{2 a^2} \psi_n(\xi) \ .\]

Defining

\[a = \sqrt{\frac{m \omega}{\hbar}} \quad , \quad \lambda_n = \frac{E_n}{\hbar \omega} \ ,\]

the equation becomes

\[\psi''_n(\xi) + (\lambda_n - \xi^2) \psi_n(\xi) = 0 \ .\]

Asymptotic behavior. For \(|\xi| \rightarrow +\infty\), the elastic term dominates the eigenvalue term, and thus

\[\begin{aligned} 0 & \simeq - \psi'' + \xi^2 \psi \ . \end{aligned}\]

The asymptotic behavior reads \(\psi_{n,\infty}(\xi) = e^{-\xi^2/2}\). This can be checked evaluating the first two derivatives

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{d\xi} e^{-\xi^2/2} & = - \xi e^{-\xi^2/2} \\ \frac{d^2}{d\xi^2} e^{-\xi^2/2} & = \left( -1 + \xi^2 \right) e^{-\xi^2/2} \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

and comparing the leading order contributions \(O(\xi^2 e^{-\xi^2/2})\).

Full solution. Let the solution be \(\psi(\xi) = h(\xi) e^{-\xi^2/2}\), with unknown \(h(\xi)\). Its derivatives read

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \dfrac{d}{d \xi} \psi & = \left( h' - \xi h \right) e^{-\xi^2/2} \\ \dfrac{d^2}{d \xi^2} \psi & = \left( h'' - h - 2 \xi h' + \xi^2 h \right) e^{-\xi^2/2} \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

Introducing this expression in the differential equation

\[\begin{aligned} 0 & = ( \lambda - \xi^2 ) h(\xi) + ( h''(\xi) - h(\xi) - 2 \xi h'(\xi) + \xi^2 h(\xi) ) \ . \end{aligned}\]

Let the power expansion of \(h(\xi)\) be

\[h(\xi) = \sum_{k=0}^{+\infty} c_k \xi^k \ ,\]

thus the equation becomes

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} 0 & = (\lambda - \xi^2) \sum_{k=0}^{+\infty} c_k \xi^k + \sum_{k=0}^{+\infty} k (k-1) c_k \xi^{k-2} - \sum_{k=0}^{+\infty} c_k \xi^k - 2 \xi \sum_{k=0}^{+\infty} k \, c_k \xi^{k} + \xi^2 \sum_{k=0}^{+\infty} c_k \xi^k = \\ & = (\lambda - \xi^2) \sum_{k=0}^{+\infty} c_k \xi^k + \sum_{k=2}^{+\infty} k (k-1) c_k \xi^{k-2} - \sum_{k=0}^{+\infty} c_k \xi^k - 2 \xi \sum_{k=1}^{+\infty} k \, c_k \xi^{k-1} + \xi^2 \sum_{k=0}^{+\infty} c_k \xi^k = \\ & = (\lambda - \xi^2) \sum_{k=0}^{+\infty} c_k \xi^k + \sum_{k=0}^{+\infty} (k+2) (k+1) c_{k+2} \xi^{k} - \sum_{k=0}^{+\infty} c_k \xi^k - 2 \xi \sum_{k=0}^{+\infty} (k+1) \, c_{k+1} \xi^{k} + \xi^2 \sum_{k=0}^{+\infty} c_k \xi^k = \\ & = \dots \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

24.1.2.2. Creator and annihilation operators#

Let’s call \(\psi_n(x)=|n\rangle\). It’s possible to prove (todo prove it!) that there are two operators

  • \(\hat a^\dagger|n\rangle\to|n+1\rangle\), creation operator, that moves the state \(|n\rangle\) to the state \(|n+1\rangle\)

  • \(\hat a|n\rangle\to|n-1\rangle\), annihilation operator, that moves the state \(|n\rangle\) to the state \(|n-1\rangle\)

These operators are defined with the following proportionality constants - whose reason appears in the definition of the number operator -

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \hat a^\dagger|n\rangle & =\sqrt{n+1}\,|n+1\rangle \\ \hat a|n\rangle & =\sqrt{n}\,|n-1\rangle \ , \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

with the condition

\[ \hat a|0\rangle=0 \ . \]

With \(c=\hbar=1\), creation/destruction in terms of \(\hat x,\hat p\):

\[ \hat a^\dagger=\sqrt{\tfrac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat x-\tfrac{i\hat p}{m\omega}\right),\qquad \hat a=\sqrt{\tfrac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat x+\tfrac{i\hat p}{m\omega}\right) \]
Creation and annihilation operators, and the Hamiltonian
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \hat{H} & = \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2 m} + \frac{m \omega^2 \hat{x}^2}{2} = \\ & = \hbar \omega \underbrace{ \sqrt{\frac{m \omega}{2 \hbar}} \left( \hat{x} - i \frac{\hat{p}}{m \omega} \right)}_{= \hat{a}^\dagger} \underbrace{\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2 \hbar}} \left( \hat{x} + i \frac{\hat{p}}{m \omega} \right)}_{=\hat{a}} - i \hbar \omega \frac{m \omega}{2 \hbar} \underbrace{\left( \hat{x} \frac{\hat{p}}{m \omega} - \frac{\hat{p}}{m \omega} \hat{x} \right)}_{\frac{1}{m \omega}[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = \frac{i \hbar}{m \omega}} = \\ & = \hbar\omega \left( \hat{a}^\dagger \hat{a} + \frac{1}{2} \right) \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
Commutation of annihilation and creation operator
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \left[ \hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger \right] & = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar} \left[ \hat{x} + i \frac{\hat{p}}{m\omega}, \hat{x} - i \frac{\hat{p}}{m\omega}\right] = \\ & = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar} \left\{ -\frac{i}{\omega} [ \hat{x}, \hat{p} ] + \frac{i}{\omega} [ \hat{p}, \hat{x} ] \right\} = \\ & = -\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar} \, 2 \frac{i}{\omega} [ \hat{x}, \hat{p} ] = \\ & = - i \cdot i \hbar = \hbar \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
Details about the action of creation and annihilation operators on eigenstates in position basis

24.1.2.3. Number operator#

The number operator is defined as \(\hat a^\dagger\hat a=:\hat n\), with \(\hat n|n\rangle=n|n\rangle\).

\[ \hat H=\hbar\omega\left(\tfrac12+\hat a^\dagger\hat a\right),\qquad [\hat a,\hat a^\dagger]=1 \]

All these results apply to a single particle, in a 1-dim domain, with a single oscillation frequency.

Creation and destruction

Motivation

  • EM energy is quantized. These quanta (photons) have \(E=\hbar\omega\), \(\vec p=\hbar\vec k\).

  • Photons can be created/destroyed. Example, \(\gamma\) emission:

    1. destruction of a high-energy \(e^-\),

    2. creation of a lower-energy \(e^-\) + \(\gamma\).

  • \(e^-\) can be represented by wavefunctions.

  • 1917, A. Einstein: re-derived Planck’s law; spontaneous and stimulated emission.

Models for creation and destruction

Single-frequency oscillators at frequency \(\nu\)

  • creation → 1 more photon → \(\Delta E = h\nu\)

  • destruction → 1 photon less → \(\Delta E = -h\nu\)

Classical oscillator

\[ H=\frac{p^2}{2m}+\frac{m\omega^2x^2}{2}\quad\longrightarrow\quad \text{Quantum: }\hat H=\frac{\hat p^2}{2m}+\frac{m\omega^2\hat x^2}{2} \]

Eigenproblem \(E_n|\psi_n\rangle=\left(\frac{\hat p^2}{2m}+\frac{m\omega^2\hat x^2}{2}\right)|\psi_n\rangle\). In position representation:

\[ E_n\psi_n=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi_n''+\frac{m\omega^2x^2}{2}\psi_n \quad\text{+ b.c. s.t. } \psi\big|_{\pm\infty}\to0 \]

A solution exists only for a set of discrete values of \(E_n\); for all the other values the b.c. are not satisfied.

\[ \psi_n(x)=c_nH_n(x)e^{-\frac12x^2},\qquad E_n=\left(\tfrac12+n\right)\hbar\omega \]

\(E_n\)

Creation / destruction operators

Let’s call \(\psi_n(x)=|n\rangle\).

  • \(\hat a^\dagger|n\rangle\to|n+1\rangle\): moves from the state \(|n\rangle\) to the state \(|n+1\rangle\)

  • \(\hat a|n\rangle\to|n-1\rangle\)

The right proportionality constants give:

\[ \hat a^\dagger|n\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}\,|n+1\rangle,\qquad \hat a|n\rangle=\sqrt{n}\,|n-1\rangle,\qquad \hat a|0\rangle=0 \]

With \(c=\hbar=1\), creation/destruction in terms of \(\hat x,\hat p\):

\[ \hat a^\dagger=\sqrt{\tfrac{\omega}{2}}\left(\hat x+\tfrac{i\hat p}{\omega}\right),\qquad \hat a=\sqrt{\tfrac{\omega}{2}}\left(\hat x-\tfrac{i\hat p}{\omega}\right) \]

[check] the usual convention has the signs the other way round (\(\hat a\propto \hat x+i\hat p/\omega\)).

Number operator: \(\hat a^\dagger\hat a=:\hat n\), with \(\hat n|n\rangle=n|n\rangle\).

\[ \hat H=\hbar\omega\left(\tfrac12+\hat a^\dagger\hat a\right),\qquad [\hat a,\hat a^\dagger]=1 \]

All these results apply to a single particle, in a 1-dim domain, with a single oscillation frequency.
