25.4. Photons - EM field quantization with Coulomb gauge#

25.4.1. Classical fields#

In free space without charge and current densities, the vector potential satisfies the homogeneous wave equation \(\square \vec{a} = \vec{0}\), whose solution can be written as a linear combination of Fourier modes. For finite (or periodic) domains

\[\vec{a}(\vec{r},t) = \sum_{\vec{k}} \vec{a}(\vec{k},t) e^{i \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r}} \ ,\]

with \(k_a L_a = n_a 2 \pi\), \(n_a \in \mathbb{Z}\). For infinite domains

\[\vec{a}(\vec{r},t) = \dfrac{1}{(2 \pi )^{3/2}} \int_{\vec{k}} \vec{a}(\vec{k},t) e^{i \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r}} \, d \vec{k} \ .\]

Coulomb gauge. Exploiting the orthogonality of the base functions \(\frac{e^{i \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r}}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\), Coulomb gauge \(\nabla \cdot \vec{a} = 0\) implies

\[i \vec{k} \cdot \vec{a}(\vec{k}, t) = 0 \quad , \quad \forall \vec{k} \ .\]

Thus, as each coefficient \(\vec{a}(\vec{k},t)\) is orthogonal to its corresponding wave vector \(\vec{k}\), it only has 2 independent components (corresponding to polarization). Let \(\{ \vec{\epsilon}_{\vec{k},\mu} \}_{\mu \in \{1, 2\}}\) an orthogonal basis - with \(\vec{\epsilon}_{\vec{k},\mu} \cdot \vec{k} = 0\) - for the coefficient \(\vec{a}(\vec{k},t)\), then this coefficient can be written as

\[\vec{a}(\vec{k}, t) = \sum_{\mu} \vec{\epsilon}_{\vec{k}, \mu} a(\vec{k},\mu, t) \ .\]

Real-valued field, \(\vec{a}(\vec{r},t)\). Since \(\vec{a}(\vec{r},t)\) is a real-valued field, then

(25.3)#\[\vec{a}(\vec{k},t) = \vec{a}^*(-\vec{k},t) \ . \]

Solution of the homogeneous wave equation. Exploiting the orthogonality of the base functions, and the linearity of the wave equation, each coefficient of the Fourier expansion satisfies a linear second-order equation (simple harmonic oscillator),

\[\frac{1}{c^2}\partial_{tt} \vec{a}(\vec{k}, t) + |\vec{k}|^2 \vec{a}(\vec{k},t) = \vec{0} \ ,\]

whose solution reads

\[\vec{a}(\vec{k},t) = \vec{a}_{-}(\vec{k}) e^{i \omega_{\vec{k}} t} + \vec{a}_{+}(\vec{k}) e^{- i \omega_{\vec{k}} t} \ ,\]

with \(\omega_{\vec{k}} = c | \vec{k} |\), and the coefficients to be determined with the initial conditions for the potential governed by the wave equation. The condition (25.3) about real-valued field \(\vec{a}(\vec{r},t)\) implies

\[\vec{a}_+(\vec{k},t) = \vec{a}^*_-(-\vec{k},t) \quad , \quad \vec{a}_-(\vec{k},t) = \vec{a}^*_+(-\vec{k},t) \ .\]
Details
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \vec{a}(\vec{k},t) & = \vec{a}_{-}(\vec{k}) e^{i \omega_{\vec{k}} t} + \vec{a}_{+}(\vec{k}) e^{- i \omega_{\vec{k}} t} \\ \vec{a}^*(-\vec{k},t) & = \left( \vec{a}_{-}(-\vec{k}) e^{i \omega_{-\vec{k}} t} + \vec{a}_{+}(-\vec{k}) e^{- i \omega_{-\vec{k}} t} \right)^* = \\ & = \vec{a}_{-}^*(-\vec{k}) e^{-i \omega_{\vec{k}} t} + \vec{a}^*_{+}(-\vec{k}) e^{i \omega_{\vec{k}} t} \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

Solution of the wave equation. Putting everything together, the solution of the wave equation for the vector potential can be written - here in a periodic domain - as

(25.4)#\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \vec{a}(\vec{r},t) & = \sum_{\vec{k}} \vec{a}(\vec{k},t) e^{i \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r}} = \\ & = \sum_{\vec{k}} \left[ \vec{a}_{-}(\vec{k}) e^{i \omega_{\vec{k}} t} + \vec{a}_{+}(\vec{k}) e^{- i \omega_{\vec{k}} t} \right] e^{i \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r}} = && \text{(see re-arrangement)} \\ & = \sum_{\vec{k}} \left[ \vec{a}_{-}(-\vec{k}) e^{i ( \omega_{\vec{k}} t - \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r} )} + \vec{a}_{+}(\vec{k}) e^{- i ( \omega_{\vec{k}} t - \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r})} \right] \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
Re-arrangement of the summation

For each \(\vec{k}\) (except for \(\vec{k} = \vec{0}\), to avoid summing twice the same contributions), summing over \(\vec{k}\) and \(-\vec{k}\) produces 4 terms

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} & \vec{a}(\vec{k}, t) + \vec{a}(-\vec{k}, t) = \\ & = \vec{a}_{-}(\vec{k}) e^{i ( \omega_{\vec{k}} t + \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r})} + \vec{a}_{+}(\vec{k}) e^{i( - \omega_{\vec{k}} t + \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r})} + \vec{a}_{-}(-\vec{k}) e^{i ( \omega_{\vec{k}} t - \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r})} + \vec{a}_{+}(-\vec{k}) e^{i(- \omega_{\vec{k}} t -i \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r})} \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

that can be re-grouped as

\[\begin{aligned} & = \underbrace{ \vec{a}_{-}(\vec{k}) e^{i ( \omega_{\vec{k}} t + \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r})} + \vec{a}_{+}(-\vec{k}) e^{-i(\omega_{\vec{k}} t + i \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r})} } + \underbrace{ \vec{a}_{+}(\vec{k}) e^{-i(\omega_{\vec{k}} t - \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r})} + \vec{a}_{-}(-\vec{k}) e^{i ( \omega_{\vec{k}} t - \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r})} } \ , \end{aligned}\]

s.t. each pair of terms gives a real contribution. Here the sum of the first pair fives a travelling wave in \(- \hat{k}\) direction, while the second pair gives a travelling wave in \(+ \hat{k}\) direction.

with Heisenberg picture for annihilation and creation operators being \(\hat{a}^\dagger_H(t) = \hat{a}^\dagger e^{i \omega t}\) and \(\hat{a}_H(t) = \hat{a} e^{-i \omega t}\) respectively.

Schrodinger and Heisenberg picture of annihilation/creation operators. Single simple harmonic oscillator

The Hamiltonian operator of a single simple harmonic oscillator reads

\[\hat{H} = \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2} + \frac{\omega^2 \hat{q}^2}{2} = \hbar \omega \left( \hat{a}^{\dagger} \hat{a} + \frac{1}{2} \right) \ .\]

Annihilation operator. Heisenberg equations of motion for the annihilation operator reads

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} i \hbar \dfrac{d \hat{a}_H}{d t} & = \left[ \hat{a}, \hat{H} \right]_{H} + \cancelto{0}{\left( \partial_t \hat{a} \right)_H} = \\ & = \left[ \hat{a}, \left( \hat{a}^\dagger \hat{a} + \frac{1}{2} \right) \hbar \omega \right]_{H} + \cancelto{0}{\left( \partial_t \hat{A} \right)_H} = \\ & = \left\{ \left( \hat{a} \hat{a}^\dagger \hat{a} - \hat{a}^\dagger \hat{a} \hat{a} \right) \right\}_H \hbar \omega = \\ & = \left\{ \left[ \hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger \right] \hat{a} \right\}_H \hbar \omega = \\ & = \hbar \omega \hat{a}_{H} \ , \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

whose solution provides the relation between the Heisenberg and Schdoringer picture of the annihilation operator,

\[\hat{a}_H(t) = \hat{a}_H(0) e^{-i \omega t} = \hat{a} e^{-i \omega t} \ .\]

Creation operator. Heisenberg equations of motion for the creation operator reads

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} i \hbar \dfrac{d \hat{a}^\dagger_H}{d t} & = \left[ \hat{a}^\dagger, \hat{H} \right]_{H} + \cancelto{0}{\left( \partial_t \hat{a}^\dagger \right)_H} = \\ & = \left[ \hat{a}^\dagger, \left( \hat{a}^\dagger \hat{a} + \frac{1}{2} \right) \hbar \omega \right]_{H} + \cancelto{0}{\left( \partial_t \hat{A} \right)_H} = \\ & = \left\{ \left( \hat{a}^\dagger \hat{a}^\dagger \hat{a} - \hat{a}^\dagger \hat{a} \hat{a}^\dagger \right) \right\}_H \hbar \omega = \\ & = \left\{ \hat{a}^\dagger \left[ \hat{a}^\dagger, \hat{a} \right] \right\}_H \hbar \omega = \\ & = - \hbar \omega \hat{a}^\dagger_{H} \ , \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

whose solution provides the relation between the Heisenberg and Schdoringer picture of the creation operator,

\[\hat{a}^\dagger_H(t) = \hat{a}^\dagger_H(0) e^{i \omega t} = \hat{a}^\dagger e^{i \omega t} \ .\]

25.4.3.1. One-mode coherent state#

The state of an electromagnetic field can be written as a linear combination of states in the Fock space, with the occupation numbers of each possible modes \(| \vec{k}, \mu \rangle\). A one-mode coherent state is defined as the \(| \alpha_{\vec{k},\mu} \rangle\) that’s an eigenstate of the annihilation operator \(\hat{a}_{\vec{k},\mu}\) for the mode \(| \vec{k}, \mu \rangle\),

\[\hat{a}_{\vec{k},\mu} | \alpha_{\vec{k},\mu} \rangle = c_{\vec{k}, \mu} | \alpha_{\vec{k},\mu} \rangle \ .\]

The one-mode coherent state is a linear combination of states in the Fock space

\[| \alpha_{\vec{k},\mu} \rangle = \sum_{n} b_n^{\vec{k},\mu} | \dots n_{\vec{k},\mu} \dots \rangle \ ,\]

with zero occupation number of any state different from \(|\vec{k}, \mu \rangle\), or dropping the indices,

\[| \alpha \rangle = \sum_{n=0}^{+\infty} b_n | n \rangle \ ,\]

with \(b_n = b_0 \frac{c^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\) from the definition and coefficient matching, and \(b_0 = e^{-\frac{|c|^2}{2}}\) from the normalization condition \(\langle \alpha | \alpha \rangle = 1\).

Annihilation operator

Expected value when the system is in state \(| \alpha \rangle\),

\[\langle \alpha | \hat{a} | \alpha \rangle = c \]
Details
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \langle \alpha | \hat{a} | \alpha \rangle & = \sum_{n,m=0}^{+\infty} b_m^* b_n \langle m | \hat{a} | n \rangle = \\ & = \sum_{m=0}^{+\infty} \sum_{n=1}^{+\infty} b_m^* b_n \sqrt{n} \langle m | n - 1 \rangle = \\ & = \sum_{m=0}^{+\infty} \sum_{n=0}^{+\infty} b_m^* b_{n+1} \sqrt{n+1} \underbrace{\langle m | n \rangle}_{=\delta_{mn}} = \\ & = \sum_{n=0}^{+\infty} c \cdot |b_n|^2 = \\ & = c \cdot \sum_{n=0}^{+\infty} \frac{|c|^{2n}}{n!} = \\ & = c \cdot \underbrace{|b_0|^2 \cdot e^{|c|^2}}_{=1} = c \ , \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

since \(\sqrt{n+1} b_{n+1} = c \cdot b_n\).

Creation operator

Expected value when the system is in state \(| \alpha \rangle\),

\[\langle \alpha | \hat{a}^\dagger | \alpha \rangle = c^* \]
Details
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \langle \alpha | \hat{a}^\dagger | \alpha \rangle & = \left( \langle \alpha | \hat{a} | \alpha \rangle \right)^* = \\ & = \left( \sum_{n,m=0}^{+\infty} b_m^* b_n \langle m | \hat{a} | n \rangle \right)^* = \\ & = \sum_{m=0}^{+\infty} \sum_{n=1}^{+\infty} b_m b_n^* \sqrt{n} \langle m | n - 1 \rangle = \\ & = \sum_{m=0}^{+\infty} \sum_{n=0}^{+\infty} b_m b_{n+1}^* \sqrt{n+1} \underbrace{\langle m | n \rangle}_{=\delta_{mn}} = \\ & = \sum_{n=0}^{+\infty} c^* \cdot |b_n|^2 = \\ & = c^* \cdot \sum_{n=0}^{+\infty} \frac{|c|^{2n}}{n!} = \\ & = c^* \cdot \underbrace{|b_0|^2 \cdot e^{|c|^2}}_{=1} = c^* \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
Number operator

Expected value of the number operator

\[ \langle \alpha | \hat{n} | \alpha \rangle = |c|^2 \]
Details

With \(\hat{n} = \hat{a}^\dagger \hat{a}\),

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \langle \alpha | \hat{a}^\dagger \hat{a} | \alpha \rangle & = \sum_{m,n=0}^{+\infty} b_m^* b_n \langle m | \hat{a}^\dagger \hat{a} | n \rangle = \\ & = \sum_{m,n=0}^{+\infty} b_m^* b_n \sqrt{n} \langle m | \hat{a}^\dagger | n - 1 \rangle = \\ & = \sum_{m,n=0}^{+\infty} b_m^* b_n \sqrt{n} \sqrt{n} \underbrace{\langle m | n \rangle}_{=\delta_{mn}} = \\ & = \sum_{n=0}^{+\infty} | b_n |^2 \cdot n = \\ & = |b_0|^2 \sum_{n=1}^{+\infty} n \frac{|c|^{2n}}{n!} = \\ & = |c|^2 \cdot |b_0|^2 \cdot \underbrace{\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty} \frac{|c|^{2n}}{n!}}_{ = e^{|c|^2} = |b_0|^{-2}} = \\ & = |c|^2 \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
Potential vector

Expected value of the potential vector field operator

\[\begin{aligned} \langle \alpha | \hat{\vec{a}}(\vec{r},t) | \alpha \rangle & = e^{i ( \omega_{\vec{k}} t - \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r} )} \vec{\epsilon}_{\vec{k}, \mu} \, c^*_{\vec{k},\mu} + e^{- i ( \omega_{\vec{k}} t - \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r})} \vec{\epsilon}_{\vec{k},\mu} \, c_{\vec{k},\mu} \ . \end{aligned}\]
Details
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \langle \alpha | \hat{\vec{a}}(\vec{r},t) | \alpha \rangle & = \langle \alpha | \sum_{\vec{k}', \mu'} \left[ e^{i ( \omega_{\vec{k}'} t - \vec{k}' \cdot \vec{r} )} \vec{\epsilon}_{\vec{k}', \mu'} \hat{a}^\dagger_{\vec{k}',\mu'} + e^{- i ( \omega_{\vec{k}'} t - \vec{k}' \cdot \vec{r})} \vec{\epsilon}_{\vec{k}',\mu'} \hat{a}_{\vec{k}',\mu'} \right] | \alpha \rangle = \\ & = \langle \alpha | \left[ e^{i ( \omega_{\vec{k}} t - \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r} )} \vec{\epsilon}_{\vec{k}, \mu} \hat{a}^\dagger_{\vec{k},\mu} + e^{- i ( \omega_{\vec{k}} t - \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r})} \vec{\epsilon}_{\vec{k},\mu} \hat{a}_{\vec{k},\mu} \right] | \alpha \rangle = \\ & = e^{i ( \omega_{\vec{k}} t - \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r} )} \vec{\epsilon}_{\vec{k}, \mu} \langle \alpha | \hat{a}^\dagger_{\vec{k},\mu} | \alpha \rangle + e^{- i ( \omega_{\vec{k}} t - \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r})} \vec{\epsilon}_{\vec{k},\mu} \langle \alpha | \hat{a}_{\vec{k},\mu} | \alpha \rangle = \\ & = e^{i ( \omega_{\vec{k}} t - \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r} )} \vec{\epsilon}_{\vec{k}, \mu} \, c^* + e^{- i ( \omega_{\vec{k}} t - \vec{k} \cdot \vec{r})} \vec{\epsilon}_{\vec{k},\mu} \, c \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

Comparison with (25.4) identifies the coefficients of the Fourier expansion with the eigenvalue of the annihilation operator corresponding to the coherent state,

\[ \vec{a}_+(\vec{k},\mu) \sim c_{\vec{k},\mu} \quad , \quad \vec{a}_-(-\vec{k},\mu) \sim c^*_{\vec{k},\mu} \ . \]
