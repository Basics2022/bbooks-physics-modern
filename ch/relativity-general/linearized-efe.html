Linearization of Einstein’s equation

6.3. Linearization of Einstein’s equation#

In this section, linearization of Einstein field equation (6.2)

\[R^{\mu \sigma} - \frac{1}{2} g^{\mu \sigma} R = \kappa T^{\mu \sigma} \ ,\]

is performed in the limit of weak gravitational field, inducing a metric that only slightly deviates from Minkowski metric \(\eta_{\mu \nu} = \text{diag}\{ 1, -1, -1, -1\}\),

\[g_{\mu \nu} = \eta_{\mu \nu} + h_{\mu \nu} \ .\]

First the inverse of the metric and Christoffel symbols are linearized, and then they’re used to find the linearized expression of Riemann tensor, Ricci tensor, and scalar curvature, to find the expression of the linearized Einstein equation.

Linearized Christoffel symbols

Christoffel symbols can be written in terms of the metric tensor and its derivatives with (6.1)

\[\Gamma_{ac}^{b} = \frac{1}{2} g^{bd} \left( \partial_c g_{ad} + \partial_a g_{dc} - \partial_{d} g_{ac} \right) \ .\]

The derivatives are first-order contributions, thus linearization only involves zero-order term of \(g^{bd}\). Thus linearized Christoffel symbols are

\[\Gamma_{ac}^{b} \sim \frac{1}{2} \eta^{bd} \left( \partial_c h_{ad} + \partial_a h_{dc} - \partial_{d} h_{ac} \right) \ .\]
Linearized inverse metric

Linearization of the inverse metric

\[\delta_{\mu}^{\xi} = g_{\mu \nu} g^{\nu \xi} = ( \eta_{\mu \nu} + h_{\mu \nu} ) ( \eta^{\nu \xi} + h^{\nu \xi} ) = \delta_{\mu}^{\xi} + h_{\mu \nu} \eta^{\nu \xi} + \eta_{\mu \nu} h^{\nu \xi} + h_{\mu \nu} h^{\nu \xi} \ , \]

implies \(0 = h_{\mu \nu} \eta^{\nu \xi} + \eta_{\mu \nu} h^{\nu \xi}\), so that the linear correction to the Minkowski metric is

\[h^{\mu \nu} = - h_{\xi \chi} \eta^{\mu \xi} \eta^{\nu \chi} \ ,\]

and the linearized inverse metric is

\[g^{\mu \nu} = \eta^{\mu \nu} + h^{\mu \nu} = \eta^{\mu \nu} - \eta^{\mu \xi} \eta^{\nu \chi} h_{\xi \chi} \ .\]
Linearization of Riemann tensor

Riemann tensor,

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} R^{\xi}_{\ \ \sigma \chi \nu} & = \partial_{\chi} \Gamma_{\sigma \nu}^{\xi} - \partial_{\nu} \Gamma_{\chi \sigma}^{\xi} + \Gamma_{\sigma \nu}^{\mu} \Gamma_{\mu \chi}^{\xi} - \Gamma_{\chi \sigma}^{\mu} \Gamma_{\mu \nu}^{\xi} = \\ & \sim \partial_{\chi} \Gamma_{\sigma \nu}^{\xi} - \partial_{\nu} \Gamma_{\chi \sigma}^{\xi} = \\ & = \frac{1}{2} \partial_{\chi} \left\{ \eta^{\xi \phi} \left( \partial_\nu h_{\sigma \phi} + \partial_\sigma h_{\phi \nu} - \partial_{\phi} h_{\sigma \nu} \right) \right\} - \frac{1}{2} \partial_{\nu } \left\{ \eta^{\xi \phi} \left( \partial_\chi h_{\sigma \phi} + \partial_\sigma h_{\phi \chi} - \partial_{\phi} h_{\sigma \chi} \right) \right\} = \\ & = \frac{1}{2} \eta^{\xi \phi} \left( \partial_{\chi \sigma} h_{\phi \nu} - \partial_{\chi \phi} h_{\sigma \nu} - \partial_{\nu \sigma} h_{\phi \chi} + \partial_{\nu \phi} h_{\sigma \chi} \right) \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
Linearization of Ricci tensor

Ricci tensor, \(R_{\sigma \nu} = R^{\mu}_{\ \ \sigma \mu \nu}\)

\[\begin{aligned} R_{\sigma \nu} = R^{\xi}_{\ \ \sigma \xi \nu} & \sim \frac{1}{2} \eta^{\xi \phi} \left( \partial_{\xi \sigma} h_{\phi \nu} - \partial_{\xi \phi} h_{\sigma \nu} - \partial_{\nu \sigma} h_{\phi \xi} + \partial_{\nu \phi} h_{\sigma \xi} \right) = R_{\sigma \nu}^{(1)} \ , \end{aligned}\]

and the contravariant components read

(6.4)#\[R^{\mu \nu (1)} = \eta^{\mu \chi} \eta^{\nu \xi} R_{\chi \xi}^{(1)} \ .\]

or, explicitly

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} R^{\mu \chi (1)} & = \eta^{\mu \sigma} \eta^{\chi \nu} R_{\sigma \nu}^{(1)} = \\ & = \eta^{\mu \sigma} \eta^{\chi \nu} \left\{ \frac{1}{2} \eta^{\xi \phi} \left( \partial_{\xi \sigma} h_{\phi \nu} - \partial_{\xi \phi} h_{\sigma \nu} - \partial_{\nu \sigma} h_{\phi \xi} + \partial_{\nu \phi} h_{\sigma \xi} \right) \right\} = \\ & = \frac{1}{2} \left\{ \partial^{\mu} \partial_{\xi} h^{\xi \chi} - \square h^{\mu \chi} - \partial^{\mu \chi} h + \partial^{\chi} \partial_{\phi} h^{\phi \mu} \right\} \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
Linearization of the term \(\ -\frac{1}{2} g^{\mu \sigma} R\)
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} R & := g^{\sigma \nu} R_{\sigma \nu} = \\ & \sim \frac{1}{2} \eta^{\sigma \nu} \eta^{\xi \phi} \left( \partial_{\xi \sigma} h_{\phi \nu} - \partial_{\xi \phi} h_{\sigma \nu} - \partial_{\nu \sigma} h_{\phi \xi} + \partial_{\nu \phi} h_{\sigma \xi} \right) = \\ & = \partial^{\nu \phi} h_{\nu \phi} - \square h = R^{(1)} \ , \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

with

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \eta^{\sigma \nu} \partial_{\sigma} & = \partial^{\nu} \\ \eta^{\xi \phi} \partial_{\xi \phi} & = \square \\ \eta^{\sigma \nu} h_{\sigma \nu} & =: h \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

Linearization of the term \(-\frac{1}{2} g^{\mu \sigma} R\) only involves the constant contribution of the metric, i.e.

(6.5)#\[- \frac{1}{2} g^{\mu \sigma} R \sim - \frac{1}{2} \eta^{\mu \sigma} R^{(1)}\]

Putting together (6.4) and (6.5), the linearized Einstein equation becomes

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \kappa T^{\mu \sigma} & = R^{\mu \sigma} - \frac{1}{2} g^{\mu \sigma} R = \\ & \sim R^{\mu \sigma (1)} - \frac{1}{2} \eta^{\mu \sigma} R^{(1)} = \\ & = \frac{1}{2} \left\{ \partial^{\mu} \partial_{\xi} h^{\xi \sigma} - \square h^{\mu \sigma} - \partial^{\mu \sigma} h + \partial^{\sigma} \partial_{\phi} h^{\phi \mu} \right\} - \frac{1}{2} \eta^{\mu \sigma} \left\{ \partial^{\nu \phi} h_{\nu \phi} - \square h \right\} = \\ & = \frac{1}{2} \left\{ \partial^{\mu} \partial_{\xi} h^{\xi \sigma} - \square h^{\mu \sigma} - \partial^{\mu \sigma} h + \partial^{\sigma} \partial_{\phi} h^{\phi \mu} - \eta^{\mu \sigma} \partial^{\nu \phi} h_{\nu \phi} + \eta^{\mu \sigma} \square h \right\} = \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

Now, defining

\[\overline{h}_{\mu \nu} := h_{\mu \nu} - \frac{1}{2} \eta_{\mu \nu} h \ ,\]
\[\overline{h} = \eta^{\mu \nu} \overline{h}_{\mu \nu} = \eta^{\mu \nu} \left( h_{\mu \nu} - \frac{1}{2} \eta_{\mu \nu} h \right) = h - 2 h = - h \ ,\]

so that

\[h_{\mu \nu} = \overline{h}_{\mu \nu} + \frac{1}{2} \eta_{\mu \nu} h = \overline{h}_{\mu \nu} - \frac{1}{2} \eta_{\mu \nu} \overline{h} \]

Thus, exploiting the result of the box, the equation becomes

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \kappa T^{\mu \sigma} & = \square \left( - \frac{1}{2} h^{\mu \sigma} + \frac{1}{4} \eta^{\mu \sigma} h + \frac{1}{4} \eta^{\mu \sigma} h \right) + \frac{1}{2} \left\{ \partial^{\mu} \partial_{\xi} h^{\xi \sigma} - \partial^{\mu \sigma} h + \partial^{\sigma} \partial_{\phi} h^{\phi \mu} - \eta^{\mu \sigma} \partial^{\nu \phi} h_{\nu \phi} \right\} = \\ & = \square \left( - \frac{1}{2} h^{\mu \sigma} + \frac{1}{4} \eta^{\mu \sigma} h + \frac{1}{4} \eta^{\mu \sigma} h \right) + \frac{1}{2} \left\{ \partial^{\mu} \partial_{\xi} \overline{h}^{\xi \sigma} + \partial^{\sigma} \partial_{\xi} \overline{h}^{\xi \mu} - \eta^{\mu \sigma} \partial_{\nu \xi} \overline{h}^{\nu \xi} + \frac{1}{2} \eta^{\mu \sigma} \square \overline{h} \right\} \\ & = \frac{1}{2} \square \overline{h}^{\mu \sigma} \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
\(\partial^{\mu} \partial_{\xi} h^{\xi \sigma} - \partial^{\mu \sigma} h + \partial^{\sigma} \partial_{\phi} h^{\phi \mu} - \eta^{\mu \sigma} \partial^{\nu \phi} h_{\nu \phi}\)
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} & \partial^{\mu} \partial_{\xi} h^{\xi \sigma} - \partial^{\mu \sigma} h + \partial^{\sigma} \partial_{\xi} h^{\xi \mu} - \eta^{\mu \sigma} \partial^{\nu \xi} h_{\nu \xi} = \\ & = \partial^{\mu} \partial_{\xi} \overline{h}^{\xi \sigma} - \underbrace{ \frac{1}{2} \eta^{\xi \sigma} \partial^{\mu} \partial_{\xi} \overline{h}}_{} + \underbrace{ \partial^{\mu \sigma} \overline{h} }_{} + \partial^{\sigma} \partial_{\xi} \overline{h}^{\xi \mu} - \underbrace{ \frac{1}{2} \eta^{\xi \mu} \partial^{\sigma} \partial_{\xi} \overline{h} }_{} - \eta^{\mu \sigma} \partial_{\nu \xi} \overline{h}^{\nu \xi} + \underbrace{ \frac{1}{2} \eta^{\mu \sigma} \eta^{\nu \xi} \partial_{\nu \xi} \overline{h} }_{ \frac{1}{2} \eta^{\mu \sigma} \square \overline{h} } = \\ & = \partial^{\mu} \partial_{\xi} \overline{h}^{\xi \sigma} + \partial^{\sigma} \partial_{\xi} \overline{h}^{\xi \mu} - \eta^{\mu \sigma} \partial_{\nu \xi} \overline{h}^{\nu \xi} + \frac{1}{2} \eta^{\mu \sigma} \square \overline{h} \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
